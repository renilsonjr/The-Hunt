## 2. The three real gaps

The draft is not thin because scenes are missing from the roadmap. It is thin
because three *dimensions* are missing, and none of them appears in
`book_roadmap.md` at all. These are the decisions worth making before any
words get written.

### 2.1 Fridan has no life — ~6,000 words

He has a sister who exists only as an address on an envelope in Chapter 14, a
job that is never named, one unnamed colleague who notices he is coming apart,
and a flat. That is the entire inventory.

The book asks the reader to feel a man being taken apart, and there is almost
nothing there to take apart. The severance in Chapter 9 costs him a god; it
should also cost him a Tuesday.

**Where it goes:** threaded through Chapters 1–8, not lumped into a new
opening. The strongest single addition is a chapter *before* the Hunt — an
ordinary evening in Nor Yesey, ending as he goes to bed on 25 August. The
reader currently meets him already inside the dream.

> **DECIDED: the night before.** Written — `manuscript/draft-2/01-the-night-before.md`,
> 1,342 words. It solves its own justification problem: the chapter is Fridan
> going over an ordinary Tuesday in obsessive detail *because he has been over
> it a thousand times looking for the joint, and there is no joint.* That earns
> the domestic detail instead of apologising for it, and it plants the sister,
> the job, and the paperback left face-down on the table by the window.

### 2.2 Uxies has no book — ~10,000 words

`story_bible.md` calls her "the story's second major narrative pillar." She
appears in one scene, in one chapter, 71% of the way through, and the reader
meets her at the same moment Fridan does.

This is the largest and best-justified use of new words in the whole revision.
She should have her own chapters, on Earth 2, interleaved through Acts 2 and 3
— her city, the noise, the sister who cried, the yard, the two years she
stopped going and got ill and nobody connected the two things.

The payoff is structural, not decorative: when Fridan arrives in Chapter 14
and asks, **the reader should already know what he is asking of her.** At
present that scene does all its own emotional work from a standing start.

Interleaving also fixes a pacing problem nobody has noticed yet — Act 2 is
eight consecutive chapters of one man alone in a flat.

> **DECIDED: third person present.** First chapter written —
> `manuscript/draft-2/12-uxies-i.md`, 1,764 words.

### 2.3 Nobody in Act 2 ever tries to tell anyone — ~3,000 words

Anser forbids him from *speaking* about it. Writing is explicitly permitted —
"she doesn't read." The draft never has him try. He never tells his sister, he
writes *ask a doctor* four times and never goes, and there is no scene where he
attempts to hand the document to a person.

Right now that reads as an oversight. It should be a decision he makes and
we watch him make — and then a scene where he tries once, on paper, and it
goes exactly as badly as it would go.

**Where it goes:** one new chapter between current 7 and 8, plus a thread in 5.

## 4. What must not be expanded

Compression is doing real work in five places. Expanding these makes the book
worse, and a revision pass that treats word count as the goal will damage them
first:

1. **Zalian's dialogue.** She has seventeen sentences in the whole book. That
   scarcity is why "You are not the one I was speaking to" lands. Give her
   thirty and she is a character rather than a condition.
2. **The Sphere's description.** A plum-sized felted blue thing with two marks
   in front and one behind, and it means nothing to him. Any more and it
   becomes an artefact the reader is invited to decode.
3. **Her name, in Epilogue 1.** It is not on the page and must never be.
4. **Anser's explanations.** Their power is that they are short, complete, and
   include the parts that look bad. Padding them makes the manipulation visible
   too early.
5. **The final line of every chapter.** Draft 1's chapter endings are the most
   load-bearing sentences in it. Revision should add before them, never after.

---

## 5. Order of work

1. **The three §2 decisions.** Nothing else can start cleanly until the opening,
   Uxies' register, and the paper-trail chapter are settled.
2. **The four Uxies chapters (12, 18, 25, 31).** New material, no unpicking
   required, and they are the highest-value words in the plan. Doing them first
   also tests her register before it is threaded everywhere else.
3. **Split Chapter 4 into three (Draft 2 ch. 8–10).** The most compressed
   sequence in the book and its climax. Highest ratio of improvement to effort.
4. **The remaining splits**, in order, 2–3 first.
5. **Fridan's life**, threaded last — it is the only work that needs the
   finished shape to know where it fits.

## 7. Proposal: who the Second Faction are

The ruling is that Draft 2 answers this. Draft 1 has already narrowed it more
than the bible's three options suggest, because Anser had to know the per-night
throughput, an eleven-thousand-year timeline, Vera Lund's address, Uxies' file
and the instant — and had to be able to reach a sleeping mind and to put a
sentence into millions of them. That is not an outsider. It is somebody inside.

**Proposal: a Balian-loyalist cell inside Earth 2.**

Not a rival civilisation and not a human society: the part of Earth 2 that
never accepted the compromise. Balian's whole quarrel with Zalian was
**presence against distance** — that a maker who watches from orbit and calls
it respect has confused clean hands with a virtue. Eleven thousand years of
extraction is precisely the distant, hands-off arrangement he despises. The
operation is what his people settled for when they gave up on arriving.

The cell has not given up. They want to *cross* — and they are the ones being
faithful to him, against a leadership that has grown comfortable stealing.

Three things this buys:

1. **Anser's one refused question pays off.** Asked which of the two gods is
   right, Anser says: *"That's not a question I'm going to answer for you, and
   I want you to notice that it is the only one I've ever refused."* It was
   refused because answering would have given the whole game away.
2. **It explains burning the asset.** Nobody sane torches an eleven-thousand-
   year operation for a better yield. You do it if the yield was never the
   point and the operation is the thing you are trying to end.
3. **It makes "never peaceful" concrete.** In the instant Zalian touches the
   Barrier it is *in motion*, and a thing in motion is legible. They do not
   want it opened — opening it kills everyone on one side or the other, since
   the atmospheres are lethal both ways. They want to **read** it, because a
   wall you can read is a wall you can eventually put a door in, on your own
   schedule, without asking a god. And a door is only survivable for hybrids.
   Which means manufacturing more Fridans, and more Uxieses, for as long as it
   takes. The Hunt does not end. It changes purpose.

> **Confirm or redirect.** Uxies III and the Act 3 chapters depend on this;
> nothing written so far does.
