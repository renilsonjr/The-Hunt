# Chapter 15 — Anser

*Draft 2. Second of two from Draft 1's Chapter 6. Roadmap 14. Third person past.
The first thing that ever answers him is the thing that is lying — and the
instruction at the end is the leash for the rest of the book.*

---

There was a room.

That was all. A room of no particular size with light in it of no particular colour, no window, and nothing in it that he could describe afterwards except the fact of standing in it — which was itself a kind of information.

Because the last time they had wanted something from him they had built him a city.

They had built a rail line and a bus stop with a correct Sunday service on it, and a building with sixteen furnished flats, and a wear-path in a carpet, and a woman in a grey suit who folded her badge before she spoke to him. And this time they had not bothered with so much as a chair.

He noticed that. He understood it as a compliment several seconds before he understood it as economy, and that was the first thing they got from him for free.

Then somebody spoke, and it was not her.

---

It was in the same place — the interior room, where his own voice lived — and it arrived the same way, and every other thing about it was different.

Where she had been warm the way a room is warm, this was simply *level*. Not cold in the sense of cruel. Cold in the sense of a surface at the temperature of the air around it. Nothing was being done to him.

**"You're not asleep the way you were,"** it said. **"This will be shorter, and you will remember it. Do you want to sit down?"**

"There's nowhere to sit."

**"No,"** it agreed. **"You can call me Anser."**

Fridan stood in the room and turned that over.

A name. Offered in the first ten seconds, unasked, unbargained for. She had gone an entire night without one and he had walked half a city for her.

"That's not your name."

**"No. It's a name. You needed one, and I'd rather you had it than spend the next month calling me *it*."** A pause — short, nothing like hers; a pause for him rather than for permission. **"You have questions and I'm going to answer them. That's the whole reason I'm here. Ask in whatever order you like."**

And Fridan, who had spent eleven days speaking into an empty kitchen, felt something in his chest come loose so fast that it frightened him.

---

He asked, "Was it real?"

**"Yes."**

"All of it."

**"The building was constructed for you. The city was constructed for you. The people in it were real people doing a job. The object was real, and is still real, and is no longer where you found it."** A beat. **"Yes. All of it. You were not ill and you are not ill now."**

He had not known how much of him was standing on that until it took his weight.

He found he had put a hand out, in a room with nothing in it, to steady himself against a wall that was not there.

"The timetable," he said.

**"What about it?"**

"At the bus stop. The six-minute layover. The Sunday service." His voice did something he did not like. "Somebody built that. Somebody sat down and built a working timetable for a network that doesn't exist, and got the taper right, and staggered the last departures off the interchange, and then bolted it to a pole on a hill in the middle of the night for *one man* to read."

**"Yes."**

"Why."

**"Because you would have looked,"** Anser said. **"And because a man who looks at a timetable and finds it wrong stops walking downhill and starts asking what kind of place he is in. It was cheaper to make it right."**

---

"Why do I remember?"

**"Because of us,"** Anser said. **"You weren't supposed to. The arrangement is that the run happens and the sleeper wakes with nothing — a good night, a vague sense of having been busy. That's not cruelty, it's hygiene; it is how it has always worked and how it worked for every one of the others. We interrupted the process while it was running. We didn't do it to give you your memory. Your memory is debris. But it is yours, and it is the reason you and I can have this conversation, so I'll take the credit I'm not owed."**

"The others."

**"Yes."**

"How many."

**"You saw sixteen doors,"** Anser said. **"That was one floor of one building in one city."**

Fridan stood in a room with no chairs and put his hand over his mouth, like a man watching a car go into a wall.

---

Here is what he would try to explain afterwards, to people who had never had it happen to them and who therefore always, always asked the same question in the same tone.

She had never lied to him. Not once. And she had never told him a single thing.

This one answered everything. Immediately, without hedging, in complete sentences — including the parts that made them look bad. *We'll take the credit we're not owed.* And every answer landed on a question he had been carrying up onto roofs at five in the morning like a man carrying a dead animal round a city looking for somewhere to put it down.

Nobody had ever been that useful to him. Nobody had ever come close.

He was, by any reasonable measure, an intelligent man. He had run a control on a bus network two days earlier because he did not trust his own belief.

And he did not once, in that room, ask himself the only question that mattered — which is not *is this true*, because you cannot check, but *why is this being given to me for nothing.*

---

"Who took it?"

**"A civilisation. Not yours. Not hers either, though she made them, a long time ago, by a route that will take longer to explain than we have tonight."** Anser let that sit for exactly as long as it needed. **"They are not what you are already imagining. They are not coming for your world; they have not been able to reach your world for longer than your species has had writing, and that is not going to change. What they wanted was information, and they have been getting it the only way that is open to them, which is through people asleep."**

"Through people asleep."

**"It's ugly. I'm not going to pretend to you that it isn't ugly. But you should know that there is no army behind it, and nothing is going to arrive."**

"They used me."

**"Yes."**

"For what?"

**"To ask her something they cannot ask her themselves,"** said Anser. **"She doesn't hear thoughts. She never has. She hears what is said, and she hears what is dreamed, because in dreaming the two are the same thing — and that is the entire mechanism, Fridan. That's all of it. There is no deeper secret underneath. They needed a mouth she was listening to. They looked for a very long time, and they found you, and the reason they found you is in your blood, and I will show it to you when you're ready to look."**

The room did not change. Nothing dramatic happened.

Fridan simply stood in it and discovered that he was crying, in the loose mechanical way of a man whose body has decided something ahead of him.

"Are they going to do it again?"

**"To you? No. You're finished; you're used. To someone else — tonight, probably."**

And then, and he would come back to this for the rest of his life:

**"That's why I'm talking to you and not to them."**

---

At the end Anser gave him one instruction, and gave it lightly, almost as an afterthought, the way you tell somebody to mind a step.

**"Don't say any of this out loud."**

"Why."

**"Because she'll hear it."** A pause, and this one was longer. **"Not the shape of it — the words. Exactly the words, exactly as you say them, wherever you are, whether or not you meant them for her. That is not a metaphor and it is not a punishment. It's how she was built."**

"That's—"

**"You have spent eleven days talking to your kitchen,"** said Anser. **"She heard all of it."**

Something went through Fridan then that he had no name for, and would still have no name for a year later: the specific sick vertigo of being told that the thing you believed had abandoned you had been in the room the entire time, listening, and had chosen not to speak.

"Then why hasn't she—"

**"That's the question,"** Anser said. **"That's the right question and it's the first good one you've asked, and I'm not going to answer it tonight, because you're about to wake up and I would rather you had it whole."**

The room was already thinning.

**"Say nothing. Not aloud, not to anyone. Write what you like — she doesn't read. If you speak about what happened in that building, you will tell her, and I don't yet know what she does with it, and neither do you, and the honest position is that neither of us should find out by accident."**

"That's not a reason. That's just—"

**"It's a reason,"** said Anser. **"It's just not one you like."**

---

He woke at 4:12 in the morning with his face wet, the lamp off, and no tremor in his arm at all.

He lay there for a long time in the dark. The parking structure hummed. Somewhere out in the building a pipe knocked twice and settled.

And Fridan opened his mouth — out of eleven days of habit, out of the whole animal need to say a thing to something — and found the first sentence sitting ready on his tongue, and it was: *are you there, did you hear that.*

He closed his mouth.

He turned onto his side and lay in the dark with his jaw shut and said nothing at all.

And if anything in the universe was listening, that was the night it stopped having any way of knowing.
