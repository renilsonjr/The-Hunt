# Chapter 10 — The Sphere

*Draft 2. Third of three from Draft 1's Chapter 4. Roadmap 9–10. First person
present. Closes Act 1 and delivers the reveal on the last line.*

---

The room is small.

That is the first thing and it stays the first thing. After a building that is larger inside than its own footprint allows, after corridors that give onto each other in ways that do not reconcile, after nine floors and sixteen doors and a track worn down the middle of a carpet by four hundred people — a room about the size of a bathroom, with a window at the far end and the blind down.

Bare floor. No furniture. No lamp, and yet I can see, and I do not think about that until much later.

On the wall opposite the door there is one shelf.

It is white, and about a metre long, and it is the kind that comes in a flat box with two brackets and a paper bag of screws. One end of it is very slightly lower than the other.

There is a ball on it.

---

I stand with my back against the door.

I am aware of my own breathing, which I have not been aware of all night. Behind me, through the door, muffled and quite far away now, a man is hitting a wall with his hand.

It is blue. A soft mid-blue, a little faded along one side as though it has sat somewhere with light falling on it from a consistent direction for a long time. About the size of a plum. And it is *fuzzy* — a felted, napped surface, matted down in places, the way a thing gets when it has been handled and put down and handled again.

It is one of those balls you throw in a dryer to take the fur off clothes.

And for four or five seconds, standing there, what I feel is insult.

I want that written down exactly as it was. Not awe. I have walked all night. I have gone down nine floors and up three, along a service path beside a rail somebody turned red for me, past a stopped watch and a child's drawing on tables in a building that eats people, and at the end of it, on a shelf that is not quite level, there is a laundry ball.

For four or five seconds I am a man who has been made a fool of.

Then the feeling comes.

---

It is the one from the bowl of apples. The hand closing in my chest. The *there*.

Except that in the room with the apples it was a bearing — a needle swinging, a direction, a *this way* — and here there is no direction in it at all, because there is nowhere left to point.

It has arrived. It sits in the exact middle of me and it says one thing, and the thing it says is not *look* and not *come* but simply, flatly, with no more urgency than a fact:

**Yes.**

My eyes go hot. I am not sad and I am not frightened. It is the physical response of a body that has been walking toward something for nine hours without being told what, and has been told.

I come off the door.

---

I go closer, and the room is four steps deep, and I take them slowly, and I want to explain what those four steps were like because they are the last four steps of the life I had.

I am thirty-four years old. I schedule buses. I have a sister who rings on Tuesdays. I am, on any measure anyone would use, an ordinary man of no particular consequence to anybody outside a small circle, and I have known that about myself for a long time without minding it very much, the way most people do.

And every one of those four steps is the most significant thing that has ever happened to me.

That is not hindsight. I knew it while I was doing it. That is the whole horror of the run — not that I was tricked, but that at every single point I was told the truth about how much it mattered.

On the front of the ball, worked into the nap in a slightly deeper blue, there are two small marks side by side.

I turn my head. On the back of it, one.

Two in front. One behind.

It means nothing to me. I want to be honest about that, because of everything afterwards and everything a great many people have wanted me to say I understood: I looked at that mark and it meant nothing at all, and I still reached for it, and I would like that written down in my own words rather than somebody else's.

---

I say, out loud, to the room:

"Will you still be here after?"

And she pauses.

I have heard her pause before. All night they have had that quality of a request going somewhere and coming back — brief, administrative, never anything like thought. This one is different, and it is longer than any of them, long enough that I hear the blind move very slightly against the frame, and long enough that I have time to notice that I have asked the only question of the entire night that was not about the object.

I want to say that I understood what the length of that pause meant.

I did not. I have had four years to be honest about it and the honest version is this: I heard the pause, and I registered that it was long, and some part of me that had been managing me all night reached out and put the observation somewhere I would not have to look at it — gently, without any struggle at all, the way you set down a glass you have decided not to drink from.

Then she says:

**"Take it."**

I take it.

---

There is no moment of contact.

That is the first thing that is wrong. My fingers close on a soft light object and there is no cool of it, no give, no texture, no weight. The ball is not in my hand. It is *in the place where my hand is* — the way she has been, all night, in the place where my own voice is — and I have about a quarter of a second to understand that they are the same kind of thing, and then it starts.

The hair goes up on my forearm.

All of it at once, wrist to elbow, a single wave, and my arm shakes, and then my whole body shakes — and it is not fear. My body has been afraid all night and this is not that. It is a physical event happening in me the way weather happens in a valley: nothing to do with me, and I am the place it is happening.

My ears fill.

Deep pressure, both sides at once, like altitude, like going under water. And out of the pressure, underneath it, holding it up, there comes a chord.

Strings. Low and enormous and held. It is not in the room; the room is silent, the blind is not moving. It is *under* everything, the way a chord under a scene tells you what the scene means, and it swells, and it goes on swelling, and it does not resolve.

It never resolves. I want that noted. In all of it, in the whole of what follows, that chord never once arrives anywhere.

And then my head opens.

---

I do not know a better word and I have looked for one for four years.

It is not that I learn things. It is that the container goes.

There is a small warm cave that I have lived in my entire life, whose walls are so exactly the shape of me that I have never once noticed I was inside anything — and the walls are simply not there, and what is on the other side of them is not information. It is *scale*.

I am the size of everything. There is nothing that is not adjacent to me. Every fact I have ever known is in the room with every fact I have never known and they are the same size and they are all touching.

And I understand — and I cannot bring back one single item of it, not one, I have tried every day since — I understand how it is *done*. The making. Not why. The procedure.

It is simple.

It is so simple that the simplicity is the most frightening thing I have ever encountered, and I am not frightened, I am *finally at rest*, and I think, with perfect calm, in the middle of a roar the size of a universe:

*I know what I was supposed to know.*

---

And underneath it. Very quietly. Going the other way.

Something is leaving.

Out through my arm. Out through the fizzing absence where my hand ought to be. Steady, unhurried, and entirely uninterested in me — a current running out while an ocean runs in.

I feel it for perhaps a second.

I have no room left to wonder what it is, or where it is going, or why a thing that has been given to me would need a channel pointing the other way. There is no space in me to hold a question. There is barely space in me to hold my name.

It is the most important thing that ever happens to me and I notice it the way you notice a draught in a burning house.

Behind me, from the doorway, a woman's voice says:

"Thank you."

---

The chord stops.

Everything I have been given goes with it — all at once, cleanly, completely, the way a name goes when you turn your head to say it. There is no fading. There is no fragment left in my hand for me to work back from. It is taken out of me the way a slide is taken out of a projector.

And there is a silence so total that it has a shape. A held breath, in a room the size of everything.

I am aware, in that silence, of one thing only, and it is not a thought and it is not a word. It is the flat physical certainty that I have just been present at the exact centre of my own life, and that it is behind me now, and that whatever is left is going to be spent walking away from it.

Then—

I wake up in my own bed, in Nor Yesey, at 6:41 on the morning of the twenty-sixth of August, with the window open and the parking structure humming and my right arm shaking from the shoulder down.

The alarm has not gone off. It is set for seven.

There is a paperback on the low table by the window, face-down, splayed open, spine up, creased white in one place, exactly where I left it.

And I remember all of it.
